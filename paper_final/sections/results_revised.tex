\section{Results}

\subsection{RQ1: Real Execution}
\textbf{Answer to RQ1.} Yes for real execution and reproducibility, within the scope of the frozen public nominal replay archived under \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/}. The authoritative nominal workflow executed 28 circuits, all in \texttt{REAL} mode, with 28 successful executions and 28 scientifically eligible outputs.

The direct evidence comes from \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/freeze_manifest.json}, \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/nominal_summary.csv}, and \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/metric_results.csv}. The workflow authority recorded in the freeze manifest is the public deterministic verification path, namely \texttt{spec2testbench verify --no-llm} with \texttt{SPEC2TESTBENCH\_DISABLE\_PYSPICE=1}. All 28 rows in the frozen nominal summary have \texttt{simulation\_mode = REAL}, \texttt{execution\_status = SUCCESS}, and scientifically eligible evidence. The extracted nominal metrics span operating-point and DC quantities such as operating point and quiescent current, AC quantities such as \texttt{dc\_gain\_db} and cutoff frequency, transient quantities such as propagation delay, slew rate, settling time, startup amplitude, and oscillator frequency, and spectral quantities such as THD and fundamental frequency. These results establish that Spec2Testbench can produce real and traceable verification evidence across the benchmark-aligned corpus.

The interpretation is limited to execution and evidence production. Successful real execution does not by itself establish that a circuit is specification-compliant or even fully evaluable under the current scientific guards.

\subsection{RQ2: Simulability Versus Compliance}
\textbf{Answer to RQ2.} Yes. The frozen public nominal replay shows that Spec2Testbench distinguishes successful execution, evaluability, and compliance: all 28 circuits are simulable, 16 are classified as \texttt{SIMULABLE\_COMPLIANT}, 10 as \texttt{UNEVALUATED}, and 2 as \texttt{SIMULABLE\_NONCOMPLIANT}.

The nominal result is directly visible in \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/nominal_summary.csv} and \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/simulability_vs_compliance.csv}. The two noncompliant nominal cases are \texttt{p04\_amplifier} and \texttt{p23\_oscillator}. The flagship counterexample for the paper remains \texttt{p04\_amplifier}. Its per-case artifact \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/cases/p04_amplifier/report.json} shows \texttt{execution\_status = SUCCESS}, \texttt{simulation\_mode = REAL}, and \texttt{scientific\_category = SIMULABLE\_NONCOMPLIANT}. The metric record in \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/metric_results.csv} shows that \texttt{dc\_gain\_db} was extracted successfully with measured value \(-160.0000000868589\) dB against the requirement \(\ge 0.0\) dB, yielding a noncompliant verdict. The second noncompliant case, \texttt{p23\_oscillator}, remains simulable but fails startup amplitude and leaves oscillator frequency not evaluated in \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/cases/p23_oscillator/report.json}.

This case provides the direct counterexample needed by the paper:
\[
\mathrm{SIMULABLE} \neq \mathrm{COMPLIANT}
\]
Spec2Testbench therefore does not collapse successful ngspice termination into specification satisfaction, and it does not collapse guarded non-evaluation into silent nominal success. The result is scientifically useful precisely because it preserves the difference between executable evidence, evaluable evidence, and compliant behavior.

\subsection{RQ3: Controlled Violations}
\textbf{Answer to RQ3.} The retained frozen bundle supports a narrow controlled-violation claim rather than a broad generalization claim. In the frozen pilot V3, Spec2Testbench achieves perfect separation on the retained 16-case backend-validation scope.

The direct evidence is \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/controlled/frozen_pilot_metrics_v3.json} and its row-level companion \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/controlled/frozen_pilot_results_v3.csv}. The frozen pilot records 16 cases with \texttt{TRUE\_ACCEPT = 8}, \texttt{TRUE\_DETECTION = 8}, \texttt{FALSE\_ACCEPT = 0}, \texttt{FALSE\_REJECT = 0}, and \texttt{UNEVALUATED = 0}. The same retained artifact also records \texttt{wrdata\_cases = 2} and \texttt{independent\_comparisons\_within\_tolerance = 2}, so the frozen pilot remains the authoritative small-scope backend-validation dataset for the paper.

The claim is intentionally bounded. This manuscript does not use a later expanded controlled-violation campaign as canonical paper evidence, because no later machine-readable summary is retained in the frozen public bundle cited here. The safe conclusion is therefore backend-validated perfect discrimination on the retained 16-case pilot, not a universal controlled-violation detection guarantee.

\subsection{RQ4: Baseline Versus LLM}
\textbf{Answer to RQ4.} No quantitative answer can be given from the frozen paper evidence bundle, because the authoritative nominal workflow is deterministic-only and the bundle contains no executed LLM-ablation result.

The direct evidence is the workflow declaration in \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/nominal/freeze_manifest.json}, which records \texttt{llm\_enabled = false} for the authoritative nominal replay. As a result, the manuscript cannot report valid generation rate, simulation rate, metric coverage, correct verdict rate, false accept rate, cost, or run-to-run variability for an executed LLM-versus-deterministic comparison. Table~\ref{tab:planned_llm_ablation} is therefore intentionally left as a pending-results table rather than a quantitative result table.

The interpretation is straightforward: the LLM layer remains a planned experimental factor rather than a completed results block in the frozen public evidence.

\subsection{Secondary Software Results}
\textbf{Answer to the secondary software question.} The software evidence supports backend functionality and reproducible execution infrastructure, but it is not used here as direct proof of analog specification compliance.

The frozen software-test artifact \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/tests/test_results.json} records three current replay configurations dated July 24, 2026: \texttt{304 passed / 17 skipped} for \texttt{python -m pytest -q}, \texttt{316 passed / 5 skipped} with ngspice integration enabled, and the same \texttt{316 passed / 5 skipped} with ngspice integration enabled and PySpice disabled. Backend support is corroborated by \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_20260724/controlled/frozen_pilot_metrics_v3.json}, which records two WRDATA cases and two within-tolerance independent comparisons. These software and backend results establish that the execution and extraction machinery functions with and without PySpice, but the analog-compliance claims in this section remain tied to the frozen nominal campaign artifacts and per-case metric records rather than to the software test suite.
