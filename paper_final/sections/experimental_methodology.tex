\section{Experimental Methodology}

\subsection{Research Questions}
The experimental protocol is organized around four research questions. \textbf{RQ1} asks whether Spec2Testbench can execute real and reproducible verification runs on the 28-circuit benchmark used in the paper. \textbf{RQ2} asks whether the framework distinguishes simulability, scientific evaluability, and specification compliance rather than treating successful ngspice execution as sufficient evidence of correctness. \textbf{RQ3} asks whether controlled violations are detected in the retained frozen pilot backend-validation dataset. \textbf{RQ4} asks what measurable benefit is provided by the LLM-assisted layer relative to the deterministic baseline. No \textbf{RQ5} on expert agreement is introduced here because no completed human-validation study was located in the frozen evidence bundle used for this manuscript.

\subsection{Benchmark and Evaluation Scope}
The nominal benchmark evaluated in the paper contains 28 local netlists listed in \texttt{benchmark/analogcoder\_pro/} and referenced by the benchmark manifests and specification files. The repository metadata repeatedly labels this corpus as \texttt{AnalogCoder-Pro}, but the manuscript should describe the local evaluation set as \emph{benchmark-aligned} rather than as a formally provenance-verified mirror of an official upstream release, because the present audit did not locate a cryptographic or publication-grade provenance chain linking every local netlist to an official external benchmark package. The benchmark layout documentation in \texttt{benchmark/README.md} identifies \texttt{benchmark/analogcoder\_pro} as the current pedagogical benchmark tier, while the example specification files label the technology as \texttt{AnalogCoder-Pro/PySpice generic Level-1 models}. This wording is the safe basis for the manuscript.

At the level of executed nominal evidence, the 28 circuits span 14 circuit families: amplifier, current mirror, comparator, low-pass filter, high-pass filter, band-pass filter, notch filter, opamp, mixer, oscillator, opamp integrator, opamp differentiator, composite arithmetic blocks, and Schmitt trigger. The local netlists are compact rather than industrial-scale: a direct audit of the 28 \texttt{.cir} files in \texttt{benchmark/analogcoder\_pro/} gives a range of 5 to 24 non-empty lines per netlist, with a median of 13 non-empty lines, and a range of 3 to 18 statement-like lines, with a median of 9. These counts are included only as a descriptive complexity proxy for the local benchmark files and should not be overstated as a universal circuit-complexity metric.

The benchmark manifests also document the analyses and expected metrics available to the framework. Across the 28 cases, the configured analyses include DC, AC, transient, and Fourier/spectral paths, depending on circuit family. The configured metric space includes steady-state quantities such as operating point and quiescent current, AC quantities such as \texttt{dc\_gain\_db}, cutoff frequency, and phase margin, transient quantities such as propagation delay, slew-related behavior, settling time, oscillator startup amplitude, and oscillator frequency, and spectral quantities such as THD and fundamental frequency. In the reviewer-revision frozen replay, the actually extracted nominal evidence is recorded in \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/nominal/metric_results.csv}, which contains nominal measurements over operating point, quiescent current, \texttt{dc\_gain\_db}, phase margin, cutoff frequency, propagation delay, oscillator frequency, startup amplitude, slew rate, settling time, THD, and fundamental frequency.

\subsection{Execution Environment}
The execution environment recorded for the frozen paper bundle is \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/support/environment.json} together with \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/nominal/freeze_manifest.json}. These artifacts report Windows 10 as the operating system, Python 3.12.2, ngspice \texttt{ngspice-41 : Circuit level simulation program}, and the source Git commit recorded in the freeze manifest. The same freeze manifest records a single authoritative workflow: \texttt{public\_cli\_verify} with \texttt{llm\_enabled = false}, \texttt{pyspice\_disabled = true}, \texttt{allow\_mock = false}, specifications from \texttt{examples/benchmark\_nominal\_specs}, and netlists from \texttt{benchmark/analogcoder\_pro}. This point is methodologically important: the paper's reported nominal results come from the public deterministic verification path invoked as \texttt{spec2testbench verify --no-llm}. The repository-level replay entrypoint is \texttt{python reproduce\_paper.py}, which dispatches that same public CLI path and rematerializes the cited evidence bundle. Internal canonical-harness or reconciliation workflows may exist for audit purposes, but they are not authoritative sources for the numerical results reported in this manuscript.

\subsection{Experimental Campaigns}
The manuscript distinguishes four campaign families and reports them separately. The first, and the only authoritative workflow for the paper's nominal results, is the reviewer-revision ACP-28 replay under \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/nominal/}. This campaign executed 28 benchmark-aligned circuits in \texttt{REAL} mode, with \texttt{execution\_status = SUCCESS} for all cases, 28 scientifically eligible results, 16 \texttt{SIMULABLE\_COMPLIANT} outcomes, 2 \texttt{SIMULABLE\_NONCOMPLIANT} outcomes, and 10 \texttt{UNEVALUATED} outcomes under the current scientific guards.

The second campaign family is backend validation and retained controlled violations. This evidence is archived under \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/controlled/}. The frozen pilot V3 summary records 16 cases with \texttt{TRUE\_ACCEPT = 8}, \texttt{TRUE\_DETECTION = 8}, and no false accepts, false rejects, or unevaluated cases in that retained dataset. The same artifact records two WRDATA cases and two within-tolerance independent comparisons. These backend experiments evaluate evidence extraction behavior and must not be numerically merged with the nominal ACP-28 replay.

The third campaign family is software reproducibility support. This evidence is archived in \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/tests/test_results.json}, which records the July 24, 2026 replay of the default test suite and the ngspice integration suite with and without PySpice disabled. These counts support the execution infrastructure but are not used as analog-compliance metrics.

The fourth campaign family consists of incomplete or absent experimental blocks. The frozen paper bundle does not contain an executed LLM-versus-deterministic ablation artifact, a broader machine-readable controlled-violation summary beyond the retained frozen pilot, a quantitative robustness campaign, or an expert-validation artifact. These topics must therefore be presented as future work, limitations, or \texttt{TBD} entries rather than as completed scientific results.

\subsection{Experimental Metrics}
The reported scientific metrics must be tied to the appropriate campaign and must not be confused with software test counts. For the reviewer-revision nominal replay, the primary results are counts before rates: 28 successful REAL runs, 28 scientifically eligible outputs, 16 compliant cases, 2 noncompliant cases, and 10 unevaluated cases. These values are recorded in \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/nominal/nominal_summary.json} and \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/nominal/nominal_summary.csv}. Derived rates over the 28-case executed population are therefore 1.0 execution success, 1.0 real-run rate, 1.0 scientific eligibility, 0.5714 compliant share, 0.0714 noncompliant share, and 0.3571 unevaluated share.

For backend validation, the manuscript uses confusion-style metrics that match the retained frozen pilot V3 dataset under \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/controlled/frozen_pilot_metrics_v3.json}. The relevant counts are \texttt{TRUE\_ACCEPT}, \texttt{TRUE\_DETECTION}, \texttt{FALSE\_ACCEPT}, \texttt{FALSE\_REJECT}, \texttt{UNEVALUATED}, \texttt{wrdata\_cases}, and \texttt{independent\_comparisons\_within\_tolerance}. These backend counts must not be merged with the nominal ACP-28 replay.

Software test counts are reported separately in \path{paper_final/evidence_freeze_reviewer_revision_20260724/tests/test_results.json} and are not analog-compliance metrics. No frozen-bundle artifact currently supports a quantitative estimate of LLM cost, LLM iteration count, LLM sampling hyperparameters, or robustness performance for the paper campaign. If the experimental-methodology section includes a compact campaign table, those fields should therefore be marked \texttt{TBD -- NOT IN FROZEN BUNDLE} or \texttt{not executed}. Likewise, provider unavailability must not be conflated with circuit failure: LLM configuration gaps belong to the method boundary and ablation limitations, whereas circuit execution outcomes belong to the ngspice-based scientific campaigns.
